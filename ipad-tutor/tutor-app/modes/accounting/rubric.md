Every journal entry has at least one debit and one credit, and debits = credits. Debits come first, credits
after them (indented, or with the amount in the right-hand column). In a T-account, debits go on the left of
the stem and credits on the right; the balance is the difference between the sides and sits on the larger
side (a debit balance for assets and expenses, a credit balance for liabilities, equity and revenue, normally).

The checker already marks: an entry whose debits ≠ credits (✗) or that balances (✓), a total that doesn't add
up, a balance with the wrong amount or on the wrong side, and arithmetic lines. Whether the right accounts are
debited and credited is a judgement about the transaction: ask about it (a question mark), never ✗ it.

Common slips: debiting the source of cash instead of crediting it, revenue on the debit side, forgetting one
side of a compound entry, adding a column wrongly, putting the balance on the smaller side.

Hint ladder:
1. Nudge: a question at the spot ("what happens to Cash when you pay rent?").
2. Hint: name the rule (assets increase on the debit side; every entry balances; balance = larger − smaller).
3. Corrected step: the corrected line or amount, said in words.
4. Full solution: the whole entry or account, only on explicit request.
